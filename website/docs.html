Quick start

Five minutes from install to your first tracked thing. Initial installation and first-use configuration.

  1. Get it. Build from source for free, or grab the app-store version (Windows, Android, Mac — landing soon; a one-time $25, with scholarships if you can't pay). Details here. You can run it on desktop and phone simultaneously; they sync (see below).
  2. Pick a theme. First launch lands you on theme selection. Lavender is the soft default. Migraine-sensitive? Pick Calm or Light. Cyberpunk vibes? Basketball Court. Don't agonize — change it any time.
  3. Set your PIN. Your PIN is your data isolation key. Multiple users can share a device with separate PINs and separate databases. The PIN is local — there's no recovery email, because there's no email. Write it down somewhere only you can find.
  4. Start with one tracker. Don't try to fill everything out. Pick the thing that's bothering you most this week. Sleep, pain, migraines, food, mood — whichever is loudest right now. Track it for three days. Add another if you want.
The pressure-free principle: almost every field in Chaos Command is optional. There are no required streaks, no shaming notifications, no "you missed 3 days" guilt-trip badges. Confetti happens when you log something — that's the entire reward loop. Skip a day, skip a week, come back when you're ready. The data you have is the data you have.

The trackers

50+ modules organized into five big buckets. Use what you need. Hide what you don't. Modular tracking categories with toggleable visibility.

Body

Pain

Location, severity, type, triggers, what helped — with a full 911 red-flag engine (MI, aortic dissection, cauda equina, AAA). Severity uses descriptive labels alongside numbers because "7/10" doesn't mean the same thing on different days.

Sleep

Duration, quality, wakeups, nap data, pre-sleep factors. Surfaces factor-to-quality correlations (caffeine, screens, relaxation).

Energy & pacing

Spoon-theory daily budget with a PEM-risk meter. Track activity exertion vs. crash-day correlation.

Vitals

Routine baseline BP, HR, SpO₂, temperature, respiratory rate, weight — with at-a-glance thresholds. Feeds the doctor PDF.

Dysautonomia

POTS/IST, BP, GI, temperature, and SpO₂ episodes. Lite-vitals capture (resting/sitting/standing HR + delta), interventions like box breathing and electrolytes.

Cardiac

Arrhythmia, chest pain, syncope, palpitations — a 15-value rhythm picker, home-ECG fields, and a 911 engine (MI/PE/VT, dissection). Cross-links to dysautonomia and labs.

Respiratory

Asthma, shortness of breath, cough, wheeze, pleuritic pain. Peak-flow zones, SpO₂ low, inhaler response, and an anaphylaxis detector (EpiPen + 911).

Pulse oximetry

Spot readings or overnight session reports from recording oximeters — O₂ score, ODI desaturation indices (3% / 4%), SpO₂ and pulse distribution bands. Feeds the doctor PDF.

Seizures

Eight types including autonomic (the kind misdiagnosed as POTS/MCAS/panic), aura→ictal→postictal phases, status-epilepticus detection, and seizure first-aid for witnesses.

Neuro

Weakness, numbness, tremor, gait/balance, fasciculations, speech/swallow — with distribution patterns (coat-hanger, stocking-glove) and MS-aware framing. Cross-lists with Joint & muscle.

Head pain

Seven episode types (migraine ± aura, cluster, tension, sinus, worst-of-life), aura mapping, baseline-vs-flare delta, and SNOOP red flags (SAH, meningitis, stroke, GCA).

Autoimmune / connective tissue

Systemic CTD/rheum picture — sicca, Raynaud's, mechanic's hands, inflammatory rash, arthralgia, serositis. Cross-lists into Skin, Joints, and Neuro.

Thyroid

Hypo/hyper symptoms, labs (TSH/free T4/T3/TPO/TRAb), meds and dose response, with thyroid-storm / myxedema / Graves'-eye warnings.

Adrenal

Addison's/Cushing's symptoms, cortisol pattern, a sick-day / stress-dose log, and an adrenal-crisis warning engine.

Diabetes

Glucose, ketones, insulin, carbs — with time-in-range (low/normal/high), distribution, and coaching. T1/T3c-aware.

Upper digestive

Nausea, reflux/GERD, gastroparesis, indigestion. Triggers, treatments, severity, duration. Cross-links to dysautonomia, food, bathroom.

Bathroom

Bowel and urinary episodes with the full Bristol Stool Scale 1–7, blood-color mapping, and a GI 911 engine. Optional, tag-excludable from any report.

Genitourinary

Voiding, retention, incontinence, infection — a nine-scenario infection triage, fluid balance / catheter output, gender-neutral pain locations.

Skin

Rash, hives, eczema, wounds, lesions, plus an autoimmune-lesion group (malar, discoid, Gottron's). ABCDE mole module, photo timeline, and a 911 engine for anaphylaxis and SJS/TEN/DRESS.

Joint & muscle

Per-joint tracking across 30+ named joints with laterality, plus a separate muscle model (coat-hanger, proximal/distal). EDS-friendly (subluxation, self-reduction, ROM impact). Cross-lists with Neuro.

Lines & tubes

Complications of indwelling devices — PICC/port/central lines, ostomies, Foley/suprapubic, NG/G/GJ feeding tubes, trach, drains. Red-flag engine for CLABSI, air embolism, and dislodged immature-tract tubes.

Weather & environment

Barometric pressure as a first-class trigger (high/low/swings/"pressure hell") plus environmental-allergen reactions, on a 1–10 impact scale.

Reproductive

Cycle, flow, symptoms, libido, and full fertility tracking — BBT charting, OPK, cervical fluid, and a multi-factor ovulation predictor. Gender-neutral by default. Includes the G-Spot scoped delete (see Privacy). Disable the whole section if it's not relevant to you.

Postpartum & newborn

Clinician-reviewed. Recovery (lochia, fundus), feeding (latch, sides, pump/bottle), and baby (diapers, weight, jaundice) — with live red-flag banners for postpartum hemorrhage, mood crisis, and newborn fever. Gender-neutral parent/feeding language.

Mind

Anxiety

Eleven episode types including panic, social, OCD-shaped, and meltdown vs. shutdown (AuDHD-aware). Somatic and mental signs, triggers, what helped, and a full 988 crisis-marker set if you flag.

Brain fog

Cognitive function on a usable scale, not a medical-survey one. Triggers, treatments, and trend windows.

Mind & mood

Mood, cognition, energy, motivation, and regulation — including rapid-cycling and the mixed-state window. Crisis resources baked in if you flag.

Coping & regulation

A guided technique runner with a timer — box breathing, PMR, 5-4-3-2-1, DBT opposite-action and more — that you rate helpful or not, so it learns what actually works for you.

Crisis support

An editable safety plan, hope reminders, and 988/911 plus US and international resources — accessible fast when you need it.

Sensory

Overload events (triggers, symptoms, recovery, shutdown flag) and a comfort toolkit, across 12 modalities including vestibular, proprioceptive, and interoceptive. Built for autistic and post-concussion needs.

Journal

Per-day entries with brain-dump, therapy, gratitude, and creative spaces. Markdown export, inline images, search.

Choice

Food (simple or detailed)

Two modes: "ate something" with a checkbox, or full nutrition logging. Pick the one that doesn't trigger you. Switchable per-day.

Hydration

Effective-hydration multipliers per drink (water counts full, coffee less). Glasses, ounces, or just "I drank water today." Counts what you tell it to count.

Movement

From "stood up" to "ran a 5K," with energy before/after. Chronic-illness-aware types (bed/chair exercises, PT). Spoon-honoring scale.

Self-care checklist

Did you brush your teeth? Did you eat a vegetable? No judgment, just a record.

Substance

Deliberately non-moralizing logging — alcohol, cannabis, nicotine, recreational/off-label. Method, context, effects, intensity. Neutral tone, no lectures.

Devices & timers

Change-reminders for CGMs, pumps, PICC/central dressings, ostomy wafers, Foley catheters, CPAP filters, and feeding tubes — with lead-time alerts, calendar events, and a warranty / early-failure log.

The Forge — build your own trackers

For when your weird body has a thing the 50+ default trackers don't cover. User-defined custom tracker creation.

The Forge lets you build a tracker for anything you can describe in fields. No code. Settings → The Forge → New tracker.

What a custom tracker can have

  • Name & icon
  • Field types: number (with units), severity scale, checkbox, multi-select, free-text, time of day, duration, photo
  • Optional / required per field
  • Default values for fields you almost always log the same way
  • Trigger associations: link to existing categories so the pattern engine can correlate

Example uses

  • Drug-trial side-effect logging during a med change
  • Specific pain locations for a regional condition
  • Sensory sensitivity tracking for specific environments (workplace lighting, store visits)
  • Stim tracking
  • Specific medication response (this med + this food = how does it feel later)

Custom trackers participate in pattern detection and can be included or excluded from any PDF report.

Sync across devices

Track on your phone, look at the data on your desktop. Both. Either. Whatever. Cross-device synchronization.

How it actually works

This is the part that matters: nothing ever touches our servers. There is no Chaos Command cloud. There is no relay we operate. Sync is peer-to-peer between your two devices on the same WiFi network, mediated by a QR code you scan.

  1. Install on both devices (e.g. desktop and phone). Both get their own local Dexie/IndexedDB database — independent, on-device only.
  2. Get both devices on the same WiFi network. The sync handshake requires same-network discovery.
  3. On the source device: Settings → Data Management → Sync → Generate QR code. The QR contains the local connection info needed to pair.
  4. On the receiving device: Settings → Data Management → Sync → Scan QR. The two databases pair and copy the data over the local network.
  5. Track from either device after that. When both are on the same network, changes propagate.
What this means structurally: Ren's server doesn't know you exist. Your data is in IndexedDB on the devices you control, and on the same-WiFi connection between them when you choose to sync. There is no account, no cloud, no third-party storage layer. The QR code is the trust handshake; the WiFi is the channel; your two devices are the only parties to the transfer.

Multiple users on one device

Different PINs = different databases. Hand a tablet to a family member; they enter their own PIN and get a separate fresh install with no access to your data. The PIN selector at app launch picks which "user" you are.

Document & lab parsing

Upload a doctor's note. Real NLP reads it. Not regex. Not keyword search. Actual medical NER. In your browser. In-browser biomedical NLP for clinical document extraction.

What the document parser does

Upload a PDF or image of a medical document — visit summary, discharge note, specialist report, lab printout. The app uses a biomedical named-entity recognition model (d4data/biomedical-ner-all, ONNX int8 quantized) running directly in your browser. The model downloads once from Hugging Face the first time you use document parsing (desktop only), then runs entirely on your device — your documents are processed locally and never uploaded.

Extracted entities include:

  • Diagnoses with section awareness (problem list vs. ruled-out vs. historical)
  • Medications with dose, frequency, and start/stop where stated
  • Lab values with units and reference ranges where the doc includes them
  • Procedures and dates
  • Negation awareness ("no evidence of X" doesn't get extracted as having X)

Optional impression reader (Qwen 0.5B)

For the free-text "impression" section of radiology and specialist reports, you can opt into a small on-device language model (Qwen2.5-0.5B) that distills the narrative into structured findings. It's off by default (a lightweight rule-based reader handles it otherwise), downloads from Hugging Face only if you switch it on, and runs entirely on your device — your documents never leave it.

Lab results parser (multi-format)

Lab printouts come in dozens of vendor-specific formats. The lab parser supports Intermountain, Mayo, Halifax (with OCR), Advent, and a generic fallback for unrecognized formats. Inline editing means if the parser misreads a value, you fix it in place — your correction sticks.

Each parsed result becomes a tracked data point with date, value, units, and reference range. Abnormal values are flagged. Trends across multiple lab dates are graphable.

Dismissed-findings detection

One of the more useful features: the parser flags findings that appear in your medical history but were dismissed, deferred, or marked "follow up" without follow-up. If three different doctors over four years noted the same elevated marker and nobody followed up, you have a list now.

Pattern engine & correlations

So you can stop wondering if it's the weather, the gluten, or the existential dread, and start having data. Cross-tracker correlation analysis and pattern detection.

Once you have a few weeks of data across multiple trackers, the pattern engine can compute correlations between trackers and surface significant relationships. Examples of what it might find:

  • Pain spikes 1–2 days after sleep quality drops
  • Brain fog correlates with humidity (if you track weather, which is optional)
  • Mood correlates with cycle phase
  • Migraine days correlate with specific food categories
  • Energy crashes follow specific exertion thresholds

Correlations are flagged with effect size and confidence. The tool doesn't assert causation — it shows the patterns, you bring them to your doctor or therapist or yourself.

Per-tracker analytics also available: severity trend lines, frequency patterns, time-of-day distributions, all-time data with no artificial date limits.

Privacy & the G-Spot

Your data lives on your device. Period. We literally cannot see it. And you've got honest emergency tools when you need them. Local-first data architecture and honest emergency data controls.

Where your data lives

Everything runs in IndexedDB (browser-based local database) via Dexie.js. The app has no server, no account system, no telemetry, no analytics. Your data does not leave your device unless you export it deliberately or sync it to your own second device (via the QR-code-on-same-WiFi flow described above — still no third party in the middle).

This means: we cannot access your data even if subpoenaed, because we don't have it. The PIN-based isolation means even if someone else uses your device, they can't see your data without your PIN.

Emergency tools — honest, not hidden

For situations where someone in your life finding your medical data would cause harm — an abusive partner, family hostility about a transition, employer scrutiny, a stalker, the situations we shouldn't have to plan for but do — Chaos Command gives you tools that are exactly what they say they are. No disguises, no cover stories, no steganography. Security comes from the cryptography, not from pretending to be something else.

  • Emergency logout — one tap from the sidebar drops you straight to the PIN lock screen. Non-destructive; your data stays put in its profile.
  • The G-Spot — a deliberate, two-step, irreversible delete of only your reproductive-health data, leaving everything else untouched. For when that one category is the dangerous thing to carry. It's a real deletion, not concealment.
  • Per-profile delete — permanently remove an entire PIN profile and its database, gated behind re-entering that PIN.
  • Plausible deniability comes from the built-in public demo (PIN 1111) — there's nothing to "catch you hiding," because anyone can open the app to sample data.

Encrypted backups

Export your data as a password-protected file (PBKDF2 → AES-256-GCM). The encryption is the protection — there's no hidden layer, no disguised files, no time-based keys. A plain, readable export is also available behind an Advanced toggle if you'd rather have something you can open yourself.

What the app DOES and DOES NOT do

  • Does: Format the information you enter into structured logs, generate PDFs, run NLP locally on documents you upload, compute pattern correlations.
  • Does not: Provide medical diagnosis, clinical advice, legal advice, or therapeutic intervention. The PDF reports are formatted templates of YOUR data — not medical or legal opinions.
  • Open source: The code is auditable. github.com/menelly/ChaosCommand

For caregivers & partners

If you're tracking for someone, or sharing data with someone you trust. Caregiver and partner sharing configuration.

Tracking on someone else's behalf

If you're a caregiver tracking for a partner, child, or someone you care for, set up a separate PIN for them on the same device. Their data stays in their database. You can switch between PINs at app launch.

Sharing reports

The PDF export is the cleanest way to share. Generate a Personal-mode or Doctor-mode PDF and send it to whoever needs it. Tag exclusions let you decide what they see.

What to NOT do

  • Don't track someone's data without their knowledge or consent. The app supports it technically; the ethics don't.
  • Don't share a PIN with someone who might use it to access data the person tracking didn't intend to share.
  • Don't use this app to track someone who hasn't agreed to be tracked. There are versions of "caregiving" that are surveillance, and they're not what this is for.

Self-hosting & advanced setups

For the homelab folks who want their data parked on hardware they own. Self-hosted deployment patterns.

Out of the box, Chaos Command runs entirely on your device — Windows, Android, Mac, Linux. No server is needed. The default deployment IS the privacy-respecting one.

If you want a self-hosted setup beyond the default — for example, running Chaos Command in a browser pointed at your homelab and persisting the IndexedDB on your own server, or building a multi-device setup that doesn't depend on the QR-WiFi sync — fork the repo and configure it however you want. The license is PolyForm Noncommercial; personal use is wide open. The code is yours to modify.

github.com/menelly/ChaosCommand

Useful starting points if you're going down this path:

  • The Tauri 2 build is set up for desktop deployment and can be wrapped however you need.
  • Desktop has a hybrid storage router: SQLite on the desktop side + Dexie/IndexedDB in the embedded browser view. The sync feature exists primarily because iOS hits browser-storage limits on Dexie alone — desktop's SQLite handles the larger dataset, mobile gets the Dexie-friendly subset. If you're self-hosting on a homelab and don't need mobile, you may be able to use the SQLite path directly without Dexie sync at all (pending your tolerance for skipping the image-attachment options that flow through the in-browser pipeline).
  • The Next.js front end can be served from anywhere; the Dexie storage lives in whatever browser is rendering it.
  • The PDF parser runs in-browser with no external services. The biomedical NLP model downloads once from Hugging Face on first use, then runs locally — the only first-run network fetch in the document pipeline.
  • Sync between instances is the main thing you'd need to rewrite if you want a non-WiFi-pair model. The codebase is open; the protocol is yours to redesign.

Will my data ever leave my device?

Not unless you make it leave. The app has no servers. Document parsing runs in your browser. PDF generation runs in your browser. Sync runs over your local WiFi peer-to-peer (QR-mediated). The only outbound network calls happen if you explicitly export data and upload it somewhere yourself.

Will you sell my data?

We don't have your data. We can't sell what we don't have. This is the structural answer, not a promise.

Does this replace my doctor?

No. Chaos Command is a personal tracking and templating tool. It does not provide medical, clinical, diagnostic, therapeutic, or legal advice. It formats information you enter. All medical decisions belong to qualified healthcare professionals.

Use it anyway if it's useful. The app was built for disabled, chronically ill, and neurodivergent users — but the design (no pressure, no required fields, optional everything) tends to work better for non-disabled users than the corporate alternatives too.

Troubleshooting

Sync isn't working

  • Confirm both devices are on the same WiFi network. Sync requires same-network discovery.
  • Make sure your WiFi network allows local peer-to-peer connections (some "guest" networks block this).
  • Re-generate the QR code on the source device and re-scan from the receiving device — the pairing token can expire.
  • Check Settings → Data Management → Sync log for any error message.

Document parsing returned weird results

  • Lab printouts in unrecognized formats fall back to a generic parser. Use inline editing to correct values.
  • Image-based PDFs (scans) require OCR — make sure OCR is enabled in the document parser settings.
  • You can re-run parsing on a previously-uploaded document if you've updated the model or settings.

PDF report looks wrong

  • Confirm you've selected a date range that contains data.
  • Check tag exclusions — you may have excluded more than you intended.
  • Confirm the trackers you want included aren't hidden in Settings → Trackers.

Lost my PIN

There is no recovery. The PIN is the encryption boundary. If you've lost it and didn't export a backup, the data on that database is unrecoverable. This is a feature, not a bug — it's why the app is private. Set up a fresh PIN, start over, and write the new one down.

Want to migrate to a new device

Easiest: install the app on the new device, set up the same PIN, run the QR-WiFi sync. The data replicates. Once verified on the new device, you can clean-install the old one if you want.

Something else is broken

Open an issue at github.com/menelly/ChaosCommand. Include: what you were doing, what happened, what you expected to happen, your platform, and the app version.
